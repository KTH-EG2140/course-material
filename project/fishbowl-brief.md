# The assessed fishbowl — what happens and how to prepare

Fifteen minutes per group, all four groups in one session (see the schedule). You work with
your agent on the projector while the rest of the course watches and interrupts. It is the
main evidence for the **Working with the agent** points, and it is deliberately live: what we
are assessing is how you work, not what you produced beforehand.

You have seen the format once already — the unassessed demonstration in Lecturecise 15.

## What you bring

- **A real task from your own project.** Not a rehearsed one, and not a toy: something you
  would have done that week anyway. Half-finished is ideal.
- **Your working setup**: the repository, the agent, the tests, the decision log — as you
  normally have them.
- **One person driving**, the rest of the group at the table and answering too. Swap the
  driver during the fifteen minutes if that is how you normally work.

## How the fifteen minutes run

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | You state the task and what a good result would look like. Out loud, before touching the agent. |
| 2–10 | You work. Think aloud: what you are asking for, what comes back, what you keep. |
| 10–13 | Interruptions. Anyone in the room may stop you and ask. |
| 13–15 | You say what you would do next, and what you would not trust yet. |

## The questions you will be asked

They are always the same three, and they are the reason the session exists:

- **Why did you accept that?**
- **Did you read it?**
- **What would catch it if it were wrong?**

Expect them after any moment where you take the agent's output into your project. "It looked
right" is an honest answer and a weak one; "the test on line 40 covers that branch, and I
checked the sign convention against the CSV" is a strong one.

## What is being assessed

From the Grading criteria, under Working with the agent:

- **1 point** — you take part: the group works with the agent in front of the room.
- **Higher** — you specify before generating, you read what comes back, you test it before
  keeping it, and you can say why you rejected what you rejected. Knowing when to throw an
  answer away and restart counts for more than getting a clean run.

Nothing is deducted for the agent failing in public. A group that gets a wrong answer, notices
it, and says how they noticed has demonstrated exactly what the course examines. A group whose
session goes perfectly and cannot answer the three questions has not.

## Preparing, honestly

Do not script it. The one thing worth doing beforehand is deciding **which** task to bring and
making sure the environment runs. If you rehearse the prompts, the questions will find it.
