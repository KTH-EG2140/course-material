# Letter to next year's cohort

The last thing you write in this course, and the only individual deliverable of the project period. Half a page, on Canvas (deadline and hand-in there). It is read: next year's students get a selection of these letters in week one.

Write to one person: a student starting EG2140 next autumn, who codes about as well as you did in August. Tell them what you now know that you did not know then. Not a course review — advice from someone four months ahead of them.

Cover, in whatever order serves the letter:

- One thing about **working with an AI agent** you had to learn the hard way — where you trusted too much or too little, and how you would calibrate on day one.
- One **concrete practice to adopt** from the start (a habit, a check, a way of prompting or reviewing) — and the moment that convinced you it matters.
- One **trap** — the mistake you watched yourself or your group walk into, described precisely enough that your reader can see it coming.

Honesty beats polish. "We merged three weeks of agent code we could not explain, and paid for it in opposition" teaches more than any list of tips. If the course changed how you think about your own engineering judgment, say how — that sentence is the one we are actually asking for.
